# The Benefits of Psychological Safety: Comprehensive Research Findings

## Deep Research Report | February 2026

**Definition:** Psychological safety is a shared belief held by members of a team that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking -- the confidence that one can speak up, ask questions, admit mistakes, and challenge ideas without fear of humiliation, punishment, or retribution (Edmondson, 1999).

## 1. Benefits for Organizations

### Financial Performance and Business Outcomes

- **230% average ROI:** Firms report a 230% average return on every dollar invested in psychological safety (Niagara Institute, 2025 compilation).

- **27% reduction in turnover:** Moving the ratio of psychologically safe employees to six in ten could yield a 27% reduction in turnover (Gallup research).

- **40% reduction in safety incidents:** The same Gallup research found a 40% reduction in workplace safety incidents when psychological safety is present.

- **12% increase in productivity:** Psychologically safe workplaces demonstrate a measurable 12% productivity gain (Gallup).

- **$7.8 trillion in lost productivity globally:** Disengaged workers -- many of whom lack psychological safety -- account for approximately $7.8 trillion in lost productivity annually worldwide (Gallup, State of the Global Workplace 2024).

- **$1.9 trillion in lost U.S. productivity:** Not engaged or actively disengaged workers in the United States alone account for approximately $1.9 trillion in annual lost productivity (Gallup, 2024).

### Reduced Turnover and Retention

- **Attrition risk drops to under 3%:** BCG's landmark 2024 study of 28,000 professionals across 16 countries found that when psychological safety is high, only 3% of employees are at risk of quitting within a year. Compare this with 12% attrition risk among employees with the lowest psychological safety levels -- a four-fold difference (BCG, January 2024).

- **Retention increases of 4x-6x for underrepresented groups:** BCG found that when leaders successfully create psychological safety, retention increases by more than 4x for women and BIPOC employees, 5x for people with disabilities, and 6x for LGBTQ+ employees (BCG, 2024).

- **Burnout-turnover connection:** Only 5% of highly resilient employees who feel psychologically safe report feeling burned out, and just 3% are considering quitting. In contrast, 60% of employees with low resilience and low psychological safety feel burned out, and 34% are thinking about quitting (Niagara Institute, 2025).

### Employer Branding and Competitive Advantage

- **92% of workers** say it is important to work for an employer that values emotional and psychological well-being (APA Work in America Survey, 2024).

- **84% of employees** rank psychological safety as one of the three things they value most in today's workplace, surpassed only by regular pay raises at 86% and followed by flexible work at 83% (Niagara Institute, 2025).

- **50% lower cost per hire:** Companies with strong employer brands (which include psychological safety as a core component) enjoy 50% lower cost per hire and 28% lower turnover (employer branding research, 2025).

- **Gen Z priority:** Gen Z, projected to represent nearly a third of the workforce by 2025, explicitly prioritizes employers who support their physical and psychological safety and their pursuit of mental and physical wellness.

### Organizational Resilience

- **Pandemic resilience evidence:** Edmondson and Kerrissey's study of 27,000+ U.S. healthcare workers (surveyed in 2019 and 2021) found that psychological safety measured before the pandemic predicted employees' willingness to stay during the crisis. A positive correlation existed between psychological safety from 2019 and willingness to "stick it out" in 2021 -- even when material resources were severely constrained (published in International Journal of Public Health, May 2024; featured in HBR, November 2025).

- **Burnout reduction:** Increasing psychological safety by one standard deviation decreased burnout by 0.72 points and increased an employee's willingness to stay by 0.63 points (Edmondson & Kerrissey, 2024).

- **Crisis adaptability:** Research shows that psychological safety, knowledge management, and organizational learning are increasingly recognized as critical foundations for resilient, adaptive, and innovative organizations, particularly in navigating post-COVID disruptions (bibliometric analysis, MDPI, 2025).

### Physical Safety Correlation

- **80% more likely to be injured:** Workers who felt psychologically unsafe on the job were 80% more likely to report they had been injured (National Safety Council SAFER Trend Survey).

- **65% see the link:** 65% of workers believe that a lack of psychological safety is contributing to safety risks in their workplace (British Safety Council, 2025).

## 2. Benefits for Teams

### Team Performance

- **Google's Project Aristotle -- the definitive study:** Google's People Operations team examined 180 teams, using over 35 statistical models and coding more than 170,000 words. Psychological safety was identified as the single most important dynamic in an effective team. Specific findings included:
  - **43% of variance** in team performance was correlated with psychological safety.
  - **19% higher productivity** -- teams completed projects faster with fewer errors.
  - **31% more likely to propose and implement new ideas.**
  - **27% lower turnover rates** within high-psychological-safety teams.
  - **3.6x more engagement** with significantly higher morale and job satisfaction.
  - **17% above sales targets** for sales teams with high psychological safety ratings.
  (Google re:Work; Project Aristotle, 2012-2016; findings still widely cited as foundational).

- **Productivity perception gap:** Workers experiencing higher psychological safety are much more likely to rate their own productivity as "high" -- 74% vs. 43% for those with lower psychological safety (APA Work in America Survey, 2024).

### Collaboration and Knowledge Sharing

- **Three dimensions of team psychological safety** -- team collaboration and understanding, team information sharing, and team give-and-take balance -- all have a significant positive impact on employee innovative performance. This was validated across 580 responses from 40+ companies with a 96.7% response rate (PLOS ONE, 2024).

- **Collective intelligence amplifier:** Psychological safety facilitates a team learning orientation that rewards the risky process of applying and adapting disciplinary skills in new ways, incrementally building collective intelligence across different dimensions and factors contributing to a problem (Robinson & Held, 2025).

- **Knowledge sharing enabler:** Environments where mistakes are accepted foster greater openness to knowledge sharing by reducing fear of exposure and judgment. Psychological safety acts as a key enabler of knowledge sharing and organizational learning, particularly in inclusive environments with leadership support and tolerance for error (Frontiers in Psychology, 2024; MDPI bibliometric analysis, 2025).

### Error Reduction and Learning

- **Edmondson's foundational hospital study:** The highest-performing nursing teams reported the most mistakes -- because they had higher psychological safety. Free and open communication led to learning, targeted solutions, and ultimately improved outcomes. These findings have been replicated many times across industries (Edmondson, 1999 and subsequent replications).

- **Software quality improvement:** In agile software teams, projects where engineers reported feeling psychologically safe to discuss and address problems quickly were 87% more likely to succeed. Teams that learn from their mistakes reduce defects, improve software quality, and achieve project goals more efficiently (Empirical Software Engineering, Springer Nature, 2024).

- **Agile team effectiveness:** Psychological safety fosters knowledge sharing and norm clarity and complements agile values. Scrum teams with high psychological safety achieve superior software quality, as team members address and enhance quality without fear, leading to timely identification and rectification of errors (Springer Nature, 2024; Wiley, 2025).

### Problem-Solving and Decision Quality

- **Better team meetings:** Leaders see the impact of psychological safety first in effective team meetings, where more honest discussion quickly leads to better decisions and fewer surprises.

- **Conversational turn-taking:** Beyond psychological safety, Google's Project Aristotle also found that equality in conversational turn-taking -- where all members have an equal opportunity to speak -- was a critical norm in high-performing teams.

- **Interdisciplinary problem-solving:** Complex problem-solving relies on psychologically safe teamwork where individuals feel confident to speak up with unique knowledge or voice dissent (Robinson & Held, 2025).

## 3. Benefits for Senior/Executive Leaders

### Getting Honest Feedback

- **The perception gap is real:** 93% of executives report feeling mostly or completely psychologically safe at work, but only 86% of individual contributors feel the same. More critically, individual contributors say they feel less safe speaking up and less valued for their contributions (Wiley Survey).

- **Risk identification gap:** Only 53% of individual contributors said they feel safe taking risks at their organizations, compared to 76% of executives -- a 23-percentage-point gap that means critical information is not flowing upward (Niagara Institute, 2025).

- **Interpersonal risk = business risk:** As Edmondson articulates: "Interpersonal risk translates into business risk. When employees are afraid to speak up, we miss out on insights, preventable mistakes go unchecked, and opportunities for innovation are lost" (Harvard Business School, 2025).

- **The "floodgates" effect:** Research documents that when one senior leader dares to model vulnerability and speak up, the floodgates of honest problem-solving open across the leadership team.

### Better Decision-Making

- **Reduced defensive decision-making:** A 2025 study published in the Journal of Business Research found that the interaction of psychological safety and authentic leadership significantly reduces defensive decision-making -- the tendency to make safe, blame-avoiding choices rather than optimal ones (ScienceDirect, 2025).

- **Including every voice:** Leaders who actively seek input from diverse team members and recognize unique perspectives make better decisions. Including every voice in the decision-making process is identified as crucial for building psychological safety (Edmondson, HBR, 2025).

- **Early problem detection:** In organizations with high psychological safety, problems surface earlier because employees feel safe raising concerns before they become crises.

### Building High-Performing Leadership Teams

- **Management team effectiveness:** Research published in PMC (2023) found that psychological safety mediates the relationship between leadership and management team effectiveness through the mechanism of behavioral integration -- the degree to which the leadership team engages in mutual and collective interaction.

- **Leader burnout:** Approximately 25% of leaders feel burned out often or always, and two-thirds feel it at least sometimes (Gallup, 2024). Psychological safety within the leadership team itself serves as a buffer against this burnout.

- **Only 27% of managers feel prepared:** Although 89% of business leaders acknowledge the importance of psychological safety, only 27% of managers feel adequately trained to handle challenging conversations with their teams -- revealing a massive capability gap that senior leaders must address (2025 research).

### Succession Planning and Talent Pipeline

- **Strategic imperative:** Succession planning can no longer be reactive. As boards confront turnover, retirements, and competitive pressure, succession must be active, adaptable, and tightly aligned to strategy (Gartner; Egon Zehnder, 2024-2025).

- **Diversity pipeline monitoring:** Monitoring the level and type of diversity in succession pipelines can provide leading indicators of DEI progress. Psychological safety ensures that high-potential talent from underrepresented groups is identified, developed, and retained -- not driven away by hostile environments.

- **Board-level recognition:** Boards are sharpening their value propositions around mission, employee well-being, psychological safety, and transparent leadership expectations (ProFound Talent, 2026).

## 4. Benefits for Individual Team Members

### Personal Growth and Career Development

- **2.7x happier:** Employees who feel safe to speak up and take risks report feeling 2.1 times more motivated, 2.7 times happier, and 3.3 times more enabled to reach their full potential at work (BCG, 2024 -- 28,000 respondents across 16 countries).

- **4.6x more likely to perform at their best:** Employees who feel psychologically safe are 4.6 times more likely to perform at their best (Gallup).

- **Career advancement connection:** 63% of employees cite lack of career advancement as a reason for leaving. Only 1 in 4 employees feel confident in their career trajectory, and just 1 in 3 know how to progress. Psychological safety creates the environment where employees can advocate for their development, seek mentorship, and take on stretch assignments without fear (People Element, 2025).

### Engagement and Sense of Belonging

- **95% perception of belonging:** Individuals with high psychological safety have a 95% perception of belonging, compared with only 69% for individuals with lower safety (APA Work in America Survey, 2024).

- **Only 30% of U.S. employees are engaged:** With only 30% of U.S. employees engaged at work in 2024 (the lowest in 10 years) and just 23% globally (Gallup, 2024), the engagement crisis is severe. Psychological safety is one of the most evidence-based interventions for improving engagement.

- **Recognition amplifies safety:** 53% of employees who regularly receive recognition from their managers report higher levels of trust, psychological safety, and a sense of belonging (Achievers Workforce Institute).

- **Active participation:** When employees feel safe in their work environment, they are more likely to engage fully in their roles -- contributing to meetings, solving complex problems, collaborating on projects, and interacting with customers and peers.

### Reduced Burnout and Wellbeing

- **59% burnout rate:** 59% of U.S. employees reported burnout in 2024. More than three-quarters (76%) reported experiencing some level of burnout, with 53% at moderate to severe levels.

- **Only 34% are thriving:** Just 34% of employees worldwide reported thriving in their overall well-being (Gallup, 2024).

- **The burnout-safety equation:** 60% of employees with low resilience and low psychological safety feel burned out. Only 5% of those who are both highly resilient and psychologically safe report burnout. The difference is 12x.

- **Protective against emotional exhaustion:** Workers who experience psychological safety at work report much more positive experiences, including overall job satisfaction, stronger relationships with colleagues, and fewer negative outcomes such as emotional exhaustion and workplace burnout (APA, 2024).

### Creativity and Innovation at the Individual Level

- **3.6x fear of career repercussions:** Employees at less innovative organizations are 3.6 times more likely to fear career repercussions for presenting bold ideas.

- **Creativity enabler:** The more employees feel that the workplace is psychologically safe, the more likely they will be to engage in creative behaviors such as brainstorming and experimentation (Frontiers in Psychology / PLOS ONE, 2024).

- **Affective commitment mediator:** Research in the Saudi hotel sector (PMC, 2025) found that psychological empowerment influences employee creativity through affective commitment, moderated by psychological safety -- demonstrating that safety amplifies the creativity benefits of empowerment.

## 5. Benefits for Growth and Innovation

### Driving Innovation

- **Meta-analysis: 25% more ideas, 30% better performance:** A meta-analysis of 85 studies found that psychological safety increases idea generation by 25% and team performance by 30%.

- **Pharmaceutical innovation:** A study of 62 innovation teams in the pharmaceutical sector showed that when researchers accounted for psychological safety, diverse teams performed better and were far more likely to achieve innovation success (2025).

- **The "hidden engine" behind innovation:** Professor Amy C. Edmondson, in her keynote at Harvard Business Impact's 2025 Partners' Meeting, characterized psychological safety as "the hidden engine behind innovation and transformation."

### Fail-Fast Cultures and Experimentation

- **Pixar's "Braintrust" model:** Pixar cultivates an environment where employees share bold ideas and candid feedback during "Braintrust" sessions -- informal gatherings where team members critique each other's projects without fear of judgment. Ed Catmull emphasized: "Originality is fragile; it requires seeing the potential and nurturing the 'ugly babies' -- the first iterations of originality, which need encouragement and care before they can grow into beautiful creatures." At Pixar, people are expected to get used to the "embarrassment" of showing work-in-progress, with colleagues there not to judge the work, but to help it grow.

- **Microsoft's growth mindset transformation:** CEO Satya Nadella transformed Microsoft's culture by emphasizing a "growth mindset," actively encouraging employees to embrace failure as a learning opportunity. Internal hackathons and the "One Week" experiment encouraged employees to pitch and develop new ideas, leading to a surge in creative solutions and innovative products.

- **Google's "20% time":** Google encourages psychological safety by allowing employees to allocate a percentage of their work time to pursue personal projects of interest -- a practice that has produced innovations including Gmail and Google News.

- **Atlassian's "ShipIt" days:** Atlassian provides employees with a quarterly "ShipIt" day to encourage experimentation, where teams build and ship new features or ideas in 24 hours.

- **W.L. Gore & Associates:** A lattice organizational structure and a culture of psychological safety led to groundbreaking products and technologies, including the Gore-Tex fabric, by sustaining an environment where associates felt safe to experiment and potentially fail.

### Calculated Risk-Taking and Breakthrough Thinking

- **Software project success:** Projects where engineers reported feeling psychologically safe to discuss and address problems quickly when they emerged were 87% more likely to succeed (Empirical Software Engineering, 2024).

- **Mistakes as data points:** In psychologically safe organizations, mistakes are seen as data points for learning, not failures to be punished, enabling rapid iteration and "fail fast" culture.

- **BP Oman case study:** BP Oman's implementation of psychological safety mechanisms dramatically increased innovation and collaboration, demonstrating applicability beyond the tech sector.

### The Innovation-Safety Loop

- **The four stages of psychological safety** (Timothy R. Clark's model, 2025 update):
  1. **Inclusion Safety** -- feeling accepted as a team member.
  2. **Learner Safety** -- feeling safe to learn, ask questions, and make mistakes.
  3. **Contributor Safety** -- feeling safe to contribute ideas and skills.
  4. **Challenger Safety** -- feeling safe to challenge the status quo and propose significant changes.

  Innovation requires reaching Stage 4, which is only possible when the first three stages are established.

## 6. Benefits for People to Thrive

### Employee Wellbeing and Mental Health

- **$1 trillion annual cost:** Depression and anxiety cost the global economy approximately 12 billion work days annually, equivalent to roughly $1 trillion in lost productivity (WHO data cited in 2024-2025 reports).

- **$438 billion in diminished productivity** globally in 2024 from mental health-related issues.

- **Employees are 2x more likely to report no burnout** when they work at a company that supports their mental health -- and psychological safety is a foundational component of that support.

- **Protective factor for physicians, women, and people of color:** Edmondson and Kerrissey's 27,000-worker study found that psychological safety's protective effects against burnout were strongest for physicians, women, and people of color -- groups that experience disproportionately higher levels of burnout.

- **15% of workers** report facing a somewhat or very toxic workplace. An overwhelming 89% of this group also reported experiencing lower psychological safety (APA, 2024).

### Authenticity at Work

- **Only 25% of employees with mental health challenges** feel they can bring their full selves to work (2025 research).

- **Manager trust is key:** Of those who feel that their manager values their identity, 4 in 5 workers (84%) feel mentally and emotionally safe in their workplace.

- **Only 50% of managers create safety:** Just half of workers say their managers create psychological safety on their teams (Deloitte research), meaning half of all employees lack the conditions for authentic self-expression.

- **95% want boundaries respected:** 95% of workers want their organization to respect boundaries between work and non-work time, which is an essential component of feeling psychologically safe enough to be authentic.

### Neurodiversity and Inclusion

- **30% more productive:** Neurodiverse work teams have been shown to be 30% more productive than strictly neurotypical teams and make fewer errors overall (research cited by World Economic Forum, 2024).

- **69% report cognitive advantages:** 69% of neurodivergent men and women surveyed -- with diagnoses including autism, ADHD, post-traumatic stress, and learning disorders -- report "advantageous cognitive characteristics" associated with their neurodiversity.

- **Only 25% experience true inclusion:** Just 25% of neurodivergent professionals experience true inclusion at work today, despite the documented benefits of neuroinclusion (EY Global Neuroinclusion at Work Study 2025, sample of 1,603 neurodivergent and 508 neurotypical professionals).

- **Psychological safety is critical for neurodivergent thriving:** When psychological safety is present, neurodivergent talent flourishes, bringing unique strengths in areas such as pattern recognition, creativity, and problem-solving. Without it, these advantages are lost.

- **Disability:IN framework (2025):** Disability:IN announced groundbreaking research in July 2025 introducing an actionable framework for fostering neurodiverse workforces, with psychological safety as a core component.

### DEI Connection

- **The "great equalizer":** BCG's 2024 research calls psychological safety "the great equalizer" because its benefits are particularly pronounced for historically underrepresented groups:
  - Retention increases by **4x+ for women and BIPOC employees** when psychological safety is high.
  - Retention increases by **5x for people with disabilities.**
  - Retention increases by **6x for LGBTQ+ employees.**

- **Disability and lower psychological safety:** Two-thirds of workers with a cognitive, emotional, learning, or mental disability and 63% of workers with a physical disability reported experiencing lower levels of psychological safety, compared with 45% of workers who did not report having a disability (APA, 2024).

- **Generational equity:** Three in ten U.S. workers reported that people in their organization who are not close to their age do not see the value in their ideas (32%), with that number significantly higher for workers aged 18-25 (48%) than for workers aged 65 and older (16%) (APA, 2024).

- **Empathetic leadership is the key driver:** BCG found that empathetic leadership -- a style that demonstrates understanding of and respect for the perspectives, emotions, and life situations of team members -- is the key driver of psychological safety and its resulting equity benefits.

### Generational Burnout Differences

- **Millennials hit hardest:** 66% of millennials report significant burnout, compared with 39% of baby boomers.
- **Younger workers feel undervalued:** APA's 2024 poll found younger workers feel stressed, lonely, and undervalued, making psychological safety an urgent priority for retaining the next generation of talent.
